Name: attest.  Extension: `.att`.  Driver: `attest` (`attest check`, `attest compile`, `attest run`, `attest --axioms`, `attest --passes`, `attest spec-count`). ## 5.  Axis 5: proof-erasure soundness

- Quantities: `Quantity.t = Zero | One | Many` (A/lib/quantity.ml:7-10, VERIFIED by my read);  `mul` sends any product with `Zero` to `Zero` (:14-24).  The three erasure rules in A/lib/erase.ml (VERIFIED by my read and S1 :204): (E1) `quantity_runtime q = not (Quantity.equal q Quantity.Zero)` (:102-104), a `Zero` binder never reaches `Eterm`;  (E2) `runtime_ty` (:182-190) returns false when the whnf of the type is a universe, a width-zero former or a proof type, where `proof_free` (:173-177) infers the universe and answers `Prop` (level zero);  (E3) `point_runtime ec q dom` is the conjunction of `quantity_runtime q` and `runtime_ty ec dom` (:191-193).
- The theorem TRACE-ERASURE argues from, stated for the gate: let P and P' be two well-typed programs equal up to the BODIES of quantity-0 subterms (every proof body in P' is replaced by an opaque postulate of the same type, tracked by `--axioms`).  Then `Erase.program P = Erase.program P'` as `Eterm.kdecl` lists.  Proof sketch: `Erase.decl` (:1427) emits `KErased` at every position where `point_runtime` is false and never descends into that subterm;  positions are decided by the binder quantity and the binder type (E1 to E3), and both are equal in P and P';  the runtime positions are equal by assumption;  by structural induction on the term the outputs agree.  `lower` and `encode` take `Eterm.kdecl` only, so the ELF bytes agree.  The proof is a Lean file in `proofs/` on the Stage F template (A/proofs, 9 lines today, S1 :215), the corpus gate is its executable twin.
- Why the gate strips bodies and not binders: proof-supplied arithmetic (assay `addLt`, `subLe`) picks the unchecked instruction when a bound proof binder is in scope;  that choice depends on the binder's presence and type, not on the proof's body, so an ELF with and without the proof BODY is byte-identical while an ELF with and without the proof BINDER is not, and should not be.  TRACE-ERASURE is defined on bodies.  A second gate, GUARD-TWIN, compiles each bound-proof program with the proof replaced by a runtime check and requires equal public values and a strictly lower cycle count for the proved variant.
- Residual trust: that `Erase` does not read a proof body through `Eval.whnf` of a runtime type that mentions it.  Types are quantity 0 and `runtime_ty` only asks whether the whnf is a universe or `Prop`, never the value;  the Lean proof states this as the invariant.

| `Bn254 Op` | BN254_ADD 0x0001010E, BN254_DOUBLE 0x0000010F | a0 p ptr, a1 q ptr | one ecall per op |
| `Secp256k1 Op` | SECP256K1_ADD 0x0001010A, DOUBLE 0x0000010B, DECOMPRESS 0x0000010C | a0 p ptr, a1 q ptr | one ecall per op |

Not in `Host`: ENTER_UNCONSTRAINED, EXIT_UNCONSTRAINED, VERIFY_SP1_PROOF, MPROTECT, POSEIDON2.  The first three are the ZK-shaped syscalls and the encoder can never emit them;  the HOST-ROSTER gate reads the emitted ELF and refuses any `t0` immediate outside the table above.  Trap story: RISC-V `div` and `rem` do not trap (divide by zero gives all ones and the dividend), so the only guest faults are unaligned or out-of-range memory and a bad `ecall`;  the executor stops with an error the harness prints as `exit=fault`, the emulator prints the same, and EXEC-DIFF compares.  A bound proof in scope removes a check;  without one the compiler emits the check and a branch to the `Halt 1` sequence, so a corpus program can only halt with a code, never fault.
